# Japanese Writing Guidance

## Public communication

The Council for Cultural Affairs' [公用文作成の考え方](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/93651301_01.pdf)
provides primary guidance on Japanese public communication. Section Ⅲ-3 covers
subject–predicate correspondence, particles, modifier placement, and punctuation.
Use those distinctions to diagnose a sentence, not to impose public-administration
terminology or style on every audience. Its discussion of reader needs also
supports choosing terminology and register for the actual reader.

## Translation notation

The Japan Translation Federation's [JTF日本語標準スタイルガイド](https://www.jtf.jp/pdf/jtf_style_guide.pdf)
is useful for consistent notation in Japanese translations: sentence endings,
punctuation, spelling, numbers, and Latin text. It is a style convention, not a
universal grammar or a substitute for checking meaning. The guide allows adaptation
and recognizes field-specific conventions. Apply a project's established rules
before importing its width, spacing, or katakana conventions.
