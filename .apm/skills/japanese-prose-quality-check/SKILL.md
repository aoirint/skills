# Japanese Prose Quality Check

## When to Use

Use for Japanese composition and revision. Use `prose-quality-check` for
language-independent content structure, evidence, and durable context. Do not
change technical claims or translate literal identifiers to improve the flow.

## Goals

- Make the text read as Japanese written for this situation, not as a
  line-by-line translation or assembly of individually correct sentences.
- Give the reader a natural path through the document and each paragraph.
- Preserve facts, nuance, and the writer's voice without turning the text
  into a checklist of possible misunderstandings.

## Workflow

### Find the shape of the text

Decide what kind of text this is and how the reader will use it. A feature
request, investigation comment, release note, and message do not begin or
develop the same way. For a substantial rewrite, work out the lead, the order
of the main topics, and where a reader needs headings before polishing
sentences. If the request is a local edit, read the surrounding passage for
context rather than restructuring the whole document.

When the phrasing or shape feels unfamiliar, consult a good Japanese example
of the same genre, preferably from the project or field. Look at how the
writer introduces the topic, moves between points, and attributes information.
Borrow the approach, not the wording. See [Japanese writing guidance](references/writing-guides.md)
when it helps choose or diagnose a form.

### Rewrite connected passages

Read the paragraph, then rewrite it as a unit when piecemeal fixes leave it
stilted. Start from the point of the passage. Let a sentence pick up the
topic or term the preceding sentence made available, then move the explanation
forward. Omit a subject when the reader can carry it over; restore it at a
change of actor or if the attribution matters. Choose a clause, sentence, or
paragraph break according to the relationship, not a length rule. Name that
relationship when the passage feels stilted: does the next sentence answer a
question, contrast with an observation, or describe an outcome? Choose the
connective or break that lets the reader follow it without reconstructing it.

Choose words that naturally go together in the intended register. Prefer a
familiar verb to a translated or bureaucratic nominal phrase when the meaning
is the same. In source-based writing, choose a predicate that fits what the
source does: 「ガイドでは設定手順が紹介されています」 for an example,
「差分では項目が追加されていました」 for a finding from the diff.
Retain literal product names and commands. Follow the project's conventions
for spelling, numbers, punctuation, and spacing rather than applying a
universal width or notation rule.

### Read it as the reader

Read the result straight through without looking at the original. Check
whether the lead fits the genre, the headings lead where expected, and each
passage carries the reader naturally to the next. Replace expressions that
are grammatical but uncommon in this situation. If two versions say the
same thing, prefer the one that sounds like a coherent piece of writing, not
the one with more signposting or caveats.

Then compare with the source: check attribution, conditions, certainty,
timing, and any term whose scope may have shifted. Repair factual changes
without reverting to awkward phrasing. If an ambiguity affects the claim,
flag it rather than guessing. For translation, use the original to check
meaning, not as a template for Japanese sentence order.
