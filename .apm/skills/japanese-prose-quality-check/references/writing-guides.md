# Japanese Writing Guidance

## Structure and revision

- Ryuhei Uehara's [technical writing guide](https://www.jaist.ac.jp/~uehara/etc/tech/index.html)
  discusses the introduction, paragraphs, and ways to keep the argument readable in sections 2–4.
  Use it to decide which background a reader needs before each point and where a paragraph should turn.
- Hiroshi Yuki's [sample chapter on writing clearly](https://www.hyuki.com/mw/pdf/trial_mw2.pdf)
  distinguishes proofreading from revision. Use its reader's-eye approach when individual sentences
  are already correct but the document as a whole still needs work.
- When a question is about customary usage, the [NINJAL search guide](https://www.ninjal.ac.jp/utility/contact/how-to-search/)
  points to Japanese usage resources. Compare examples in a similar genre; frequency alone does not
  make a form appropriate or mandatory.

## Examples by genre

- [VOICEVOX Engine feature request](https://github.com/VOICEVOX/voicevox_engine/issues/1798):
  a small request follows the repository's headings to state the requested settings,
  their benefit, and the version or context. Borrow the scale and fit to the local format.
- [Yahoo! JAPAN Tech Blog investigation](https://techblog.yahoo.co.jp/entry/2023071030428075/):
  the observed result creates the question, the simulation addresses it, and
  the production comparison puts the findings in context. The sequence follows
  the argument, not the order in which the writer gathered sources.
- [VOICEVOX how-to](https://github.com/VOICEVOX/voicevox/blob/main/public/howtouse.md):
  headings name tasks the reader can perform, and each section begins from that task.
  Use examples like this to find an appropriate form, not as a universal template.

## Public communication

The Council for Cultural Affairs' [公用文作成の考え方](https://www.bunka.go.jp/seisaku/bunkashingikai/kokugo/hokoku/pdf/93651301_01.pdf)
provides primary guidance on Japanese public communication. Section Ⅲ-3 covers
subject–predicate correspondence, particles, modifier placement, and punctuation.
Use those distinctions to diagnose a sentence, not to impose public-administration
terminology or style on every audience. Its discussion of reader needs also
supports choosing terminology and register for the actual reader.

## Translation notation

The Japan Translation Federation's [JTF日本語標準スタイルガイド](https://www.jtf.jp/pdf/jtf_style_guide.pdf)
is useful for consistent notation in Japanese translations: sentence endings,
punctuation, spelling, numbers, and Latin text. It is a style convention, not a
universal grammar or a substitute for checking meaning. The guide allows adaptation
and recognizes field-specific conventions. Apply a project's established rules
before importing its width, spacing, or katakana conventions.
